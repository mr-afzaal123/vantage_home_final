Corner Sofa, 3-Seater or Sofa Bed – What's the Difference?

Belgian and Dutch shoppers use different words for the same living room decision. Here's a plain-language answer: a corner sofa (also called an L-shape sofa, sectional sofa, or hoekbank/hoeksalon in Dutch, canapé d'angle in French) wraps around a corner and seats 5 to 7 people — ideal for open-plan or larger living rooms.

A 3-seater sofa (driezitsbank, canapé 3 places) is a straight single-line sofa that fits more easily into compact apartments and smaller rooms. A sofa bed (slaapbank, canapé convertible) folds out into a sleeping surface, doubling as a guest bed or an everyday bed in studio flats.

In short: Choose a corner sofa for space and family living, a 3-seater for compact rooms, and a sofa bed if you need the room to double as a guest bedroom.

Sofa Sizes & Fitting Your Room

Before buying, measure your living room in both directions and mark the sofa's footprint on the floor with tape — the single most useful step, and one most buyers skip. Here's what each format typically needs:

Leave at least 60–90cm of walking space in front of the sofa, and check door widths and stairwells before ordering — especially for a 7-seater corner sofa, which arrives in large sections.

2026 Sofa Trends: Fabric, Shape & Colour

Belgian and Dutch living rooms in 2026 are leaning into two directions at once: softer, organic curves on arms and backrests, paired with contrast detailing such as slim gold or metal legs against a neutral fabric. Neutral tones — grey, beige, cognac — remain the safest long-term choice, while a single statement-colour sofa can act as the room's focal point.

Fabric (stof): hard-wearing, easier to clean, practical for households with children or pets.

Velvet (fluweel/velours): soft touch and rich colour depth, best suited to lower-traffic formal living rooms.

Gold or brushed-metal accent legs are one of the most requested details this year — a small design choice that lifts a simple 3-seater into a more elevated look without the price of a fully custom sofa.

Sofa Beds: Storage & Everyday Comfort

If you searched "slaapbank met opbergruimte" or "canapé convertible avec rangement," here's what actually matters: a sofa bed you can sleep on every night — not just for occasional guests — needs a proper mattress layer built into the base, not just the seat cushions folded flat. Look for a dedicated foam or spring mattress a few centimetres thick.

Under-seat storage is the other feature worth prioritising: it holds bedding, pillows or spare linens without needing a separate chest of drawers — especially useful in smaller Belgian and Dutch apartments where every square metre counts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a corner sofa and a 3-seater sofa?

A corner sofa wraps around a corner and seats 5–7 people, suited to larger or open-plan rooms. A 3-seater is a straight sofa seating around 3, better for compact spaces. Both are available as fixed sofas or sofa beds.

What sizes do sofas come in?

A 3-seater is roughly 190–220cm wide. A corner/L-shape sofa runs 250–300cm+ along its longest side. Sofa beds open out to around 140–190x190cm, similar to a double or king mattress.

Is a sofa bed comfortable for everyday sleeping?

Yes, if it has a real mattress layer rather than just folded cushions. Look for a dedicated foam or spring base and, ideally, built-in storage for bedding, making it suitable for daily use.

Which sofa fabric is best, velvet or standard fabric?

Velvet is soft and rich in colour but marks more easily; standard fabric is more wear-resistant and easier to maintain, better for households with kids or pets. Both come in neutral tones that suit most interiors.
